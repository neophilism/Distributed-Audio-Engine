# Portable shared-clock estimation

`DisciplinedClock` estimates common timeline time from four timestamps carried by the existing signed-control and authenticated E2EE payload adapters. The primary endpoint must be a trusted time source; signing a timestamp does not prove the endpoint clock is accurate. Recipient identity, control delivery and secure channel establishment remain separate. This module introduces no key agreement or unauthenticated clock fallback.

Each request has a fresh random challenge and a local monotonic send time; the response binds the challenge to primary receive/send timestamps and stated reference uncertainty. The receiver captures its arrival time before asynchronous verification/decoding, rejects forged/unmatched/superseded responses, bounds elapsed/processing times and adds network-delay, timestamp-resolution and configured drift uncertainty. Offset/delay calculations use the standard four-timestamp relationship described in [RFC 5905 section 8](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5905.html#section-8); this is not a complete NTP implementation or wire protocol.

Up to eight recent intervals are intersected conservatively. Conflicting responses cannot enter the history. Readiness requires enough fresh samples; stale or excessively uncertain estimates fail closed. An optional drift estimate is diagnostic; it does not shrink the conservative frequency-error bounds. `nowUnixMs` never steps backwards: a correction outside the prior returned time's supported interval requires the caller to stop playback, reset and rejoin. Resume after a long suspension also requires fresh samples. A separate trusted validation clock checks signed-control expiry during initial acquisition.

The clock can supply the portable timeline player's time adapter only within a chosen uncertainty policy. Playback leases and qualifying measurements must account for that uncertainty conservatively. Network scheduling does not measure Bluetooth buffering, acoustic emission, room attribution or dBA. Android/iOS clock/sink integration, route delay profiles and actual acoustic timing remain unfinished parts of DAE-11 and its native prerequisites.

Six portable tests use synthetic reference timestamps to verify offset/drift bounds, freshness, uncertainty, inconsistent/impossible data, signatures/challenges, cancellation and correction behavior. No hardware/calibration gate is satisfied by these calculations.
